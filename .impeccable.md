## Design Context

### Users
High-value service business founders and CEOs ($2M–$50M revenue) evaluating a fractional CMO. They are time-poor, outcome-obsessed, and have been burned by agencies before. They browse on desktop during office hours. The job to be done: quickly assess if Marcus is the calibre of person who can actually move their numbers.

### Brand Personality
Direct, data-forward, no-nonsense. Operator energy. Numbers, outcomes, methodology. No fluff. The kind of person who sends a one-line email and it lands. Anti-patterns: agency soft-sell, vague promise language, "we help you grow" platitudes.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark editorial. Think Bloomberg Terminal meets strategy consultancy leave-behind. Not "startup SaaS" dark — that's glowing cyan on charcoal with rounded cards. This is sharper: near-black with warm tint, tight grotesque headings, tabular numbers, hairline dividers. The palette uses OKLCH — emerald CTA (`oklch(67% 0.18 162)`) is the sole accent, used sparingly.

The GrowthOS diagnostic form should feel like the same product: same typographic voice, shared CTA colour, explicit Marcus attribution at the top. Light paper background is correct for form completion context — don't flip it dark. Bridge through shared fonts and colour, not shared theme.

### Design Principles
1. Numbers over claims. If you can say it with a number, don't use prose.
2. One accent. The emerald is sacred. Every other colour serves contrast or recedes.
3. No selling. The site demonstrates competence, it doesn't pitch. Tone stays declarative.
4. Same brand end-to-end. GrowthOS form is Marcus's product — it should feel that way immediately.
5. Portrait earns trust faster than copy. The headshot is a conversion asset, not decoration.
