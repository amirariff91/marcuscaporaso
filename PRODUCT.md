# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Perth patients considering bariatric surgery — typically adults aged 30–60 with a BMI over 30, who have been struggling with weight for years and are at an emotional turning point. They are anxious, research-heavy, and often embarrassed. They have read conflicting information online and want clear, honest answers from a team they can trust before making a high-stakes medical decision. Secondary users: GPs and referring doctors who need a fast, credible pathway to send patients.

## Product Purpose

The Obesity Surgery WA website rebuild is a patient acquisition system, not a brochure. It exists to route high-intent Perth patients to the right procedure page, cost information, or booking path — and to give them enough trust signals to take the next step without leaving. Success is a consultation booked, not a page viewed.

The osw.marcuscaporaso.com subdomain is Marcus Caporaso's rebuild audit and content preview, showing the client exactly what the rebuilt site will look like: full content pages, clear architecture, and a credible patient experience.

## Brand Personality

Trusted, honest, warm. Clinical without being cold. Like a good GP who happens to be a specialist: they give you the real picture, explain the risks, and help you feel like you are in the right hands.

## Anti-references

- Generic Australian healthcare branding: stock photos, teal-and-white palettes, corporate warmth
- Pushy before/after weight loss marketing with guarantees and celebrity-style testimonials
- Cluttered WordPress medical sites with too many CTAs and too little clarity
- American bariatric surgery sites (too promotional, AHPRA-non-compliant framing)

## Design Principles

1. **Evidence before reassurance.** Every claim has a basis. No hollow positive framing; honest risk disclosures earn trust faster than spin.
2. **Route, don't broadcast.** Every section has one job: move the patient to the next right step. Not general awareness — a specific action.
3. **Medical credibility through restraint.** The design should feel like a well-run specialist clinic, not a wellness brand. Clean, precise, unhurried.
4. **Patient anxiety is a feature, not a bug.** The copy and UX should acknowledge that this is a big decision. Meeting the patient where they are earns trust.
5. **Hierarchy over decoration.** Information architecture first, visual interest second.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. High contrast text essential — patients include older adults. No motion that cannot be reduced. Medical content must be scannable (clear headings, bullets) not buried in long prose blocks.
