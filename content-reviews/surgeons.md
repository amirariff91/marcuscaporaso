# Our Bariatric Surgeons Perth — first-round review

> **Correction — 12 August 2026.** OSWA (via Marcus, 10 Aug) has since confirmed the hospitals as **Mount Private + Waikiki Private** and the clinics as **Booragoon + Subiaco/West Leederville** — matching this draft. Findings 6 and 7 are therefore **withdrawn**: the draft's locations were right, and the stale facts sit on the live OSWA site, not in the writer's copy. Finding 13's "live site says Exercise Therapist" evidence could not be re-verified and is retracted; the title remains an open credential question for Wendy (the writer reports Wendy supplied "exercise physiologist"). Everything else — the Lehon typo, the unfinished sections, the substantiation gates — stands.

**Verdict: Rework** · 11 blocking · 10 should-fix · 2 minor

This is the first review of this page. The clinical instincts are good — the comparison guidance, the honest risk framing and the "no surgeon can promise a result" position are all the right calls for a surgeons page. What holds it back is that several sections were never finished and the experience claims need substantiating. (This review originally also faulted the clinic and hospital names — those findings are withdrawn per the 12 Aug correction above; the draft had them right.) The BMI framing (35+, or 30+ with a related condition) is correct and is not a finding.

One thing to flag before the detail: **this page names Dr Leon Cohen, and our briefs name Dr Stephen Watson.** We checked the live OSWA site and it is internally inconsistent — the About page's team section names Associate Professor Harsha Chandraratna and Dr Leon Cohen, while the booking form on that same page offers Dr Harsha Chandraratna and Dr Stephen Watson as practitioners and does not offer Dr Cohen. So we cannot settle this from the public site, and nothing below asks the writer to remove Dr Cohen. Only OSWA can confirm the current roster — see the note at finding 4.

15. **Should-fix** — `Most bariatric procedures attract a Medicare item number, and patients with appropriate private hospital cover typically pay a gap rather than the full fee.` and `you'll receive a written, itemised estimate before making any commitment so you know exactly what you're facing, with no surprises.` — a Medicare item number does not itself establish hospital cover, and "no surprises" is an absolute assurance about cost.

    **Writer edit:** `Most bariatric procedures have a Medicare item number, but Medicare rebates and private hospital cover are separate systems. Your out-of-pocket cost depends on the procedure, your cover and your circumstances. You will receive a written, itemised estimate before you commit to anything.`
