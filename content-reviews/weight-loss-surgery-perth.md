# Weight Loss Surgery Perth — content review

**Verdict: Rework** · 3 must-fix · 10 should-fix · 3 minor

**Why:** Medication suitability, safety/outcome wording, page positioning, cost treatment and search coverage need correction before publication.

**Length:** current body 1,958 words vs brief target 1,100-1,400 words, including FAQs.

## Must fix before publish

1. **Medication/BMI eligibility is too absolute**

> Medical weight management options including GLP-1s may be suitable if you have a BMI of 30 or higher.

> Non-surgical medical weight management may be worth discussing from a BMI of 27 or above, particularly where weight-related health conditions are present. Medication suitability depends on the specific medicine, your medical history and current Australian prescribing guidance, while surgical Medicare eligibility is assessed separately.

Why it matters: The specific BMI 30+ suitability claim is too direct and should be framed as an assessment conversation, especially where medication suitability and surgical Medicare eligibility are different questions.

2. **The surgery definition creates an unbalanced success claim**

> These are all well established procedures with long track records of weight loss success in Australia.

> These procedures have been used in Australia for many years, but the likely benefits, risks and suitability vary by person and should be discussed with your surgeon.

Why it matters: "Long track records of weight loss success" turns an explanatory definition into a broad promotional outcome claim.

3. **The dietitian FAQ makes an unsupported clinical outcome claim**

> Patients who engage with dietitian support consistently achieve better outcomes, and because our dietitians are in-house, they can work closely with your surgeon throughout your care.

> Dietitian support can help you prepare for treatment, protect your nutrition and build habits that support long-term weight management; because our dietitians are in-house, they can work closely with your surgeon throughout your care.

Why it matters: The rewrite explains the role of dietitian support without making a comparative clinical outcome claim.

## Should fix

1. **The primary keyword is not placed strongly enough in the H1 or first 100 words**

> If you have spent years trying to lose weight and keep it off, you are not the problem, and you are not alone.

> If you are researching weight loss surgery Perth options, Obesity Surgery WA can help you compare surgical, medical and dietitian-led pathways without pressure.

Why it matters: The primary keyword should appear early and naturally so the page's topic is immediately clear.

2. **The page reads too much like the bariatric-surgery hub**

> The most common procedures we perform in our Perth weight loss clinics are the gastric sleeve ('sleeve gastrectomy') and the gastric bypass, with the adjustable gastric band a viable option for some people.

> Surgical options such as gastric sleeve, gastric bypass and gastric band may be part of the discussion, but this page starts one step earlier: helping you work out whether surgery, medical management, dietitian-led support or another pathway is the right starting point.

Why it matters: This page should be the early-stage spectrum-of-care page, not another procedure menu.

3. **The required stats bar is missing**

> Obesity Surgery WA is a specialist team of surgeons, physicians, dietitians, and exercise physiologists who have been supporting weight loss in Perth for over 20 years.

> Obesity Surgery WA brings together surgeons, physicians, Accredited Practising Dietitians and exercise physiologists, with 20+ years supporting weight loss in Perth, clinics in Booragoon and Mandurah, and medical-team conversations available from a starting BMI of 27+ where clinically appropriate.

Why it matters: The opening needs the factual anchors from the brief and should use one consistent location convention.

4. **The eligibility section should separate surgical thresholds from medical management**

> You may be eligible for weight loss surgery if you have a BMI of 40 or higher, or 35 or higher with a weight-related health condition such as type 2 diabetes or high blood pressure, and where diet and exercise alone have not produced long-term results.

> A BMI of 27 or above, particularly with a weight-related health condition, may be enough to start a conversation with our medical team about non-surgical options. Surgical options are usually discussed at higher BMI ranges; current MBS guidance for clinically severe obesity generally refers to BMI 40+ or BMI 35+ with major co-morbidities, with suitability assessed by your surgeon.

Why it matters: The page's differentiator is the whole treatment spectrum, so surgical criteria and medical-management entry points should not be blurred.

5. **Current Google People-Also-Ask questions checked 10 Jul 2026 need a direct Medicare FAQ**

> It allows Medicare rebates to apply to your specialist consultation fee, reducing your out-of-pocket cost for the appointment.

> Is weight loss surgery covered by Medicare in Australia? Medicare rebates may apply to eligible specialist consultations and some bariatric surgery items when current MBS criteria are met. Private health cover, waiting periods, hospital excess and anaesthetist fees still affect out-of-pocket costs, so our team gives you an itemised estimate before you make any decision.

Why it matters: Medicare coverage needs a direct answer, not only a referral note.

6. **The cost section is too surgery-only and includes inducement-adjacent finance copy**

> The cost of weight loss surgery in Perth varies depending on the procedure, your level of private health cover, your hospital excess, and anaesthetist fees. Financing options such as Zip and TLC are also available for eligible patients.

> The cost of weight loss treatment in Perth depends on the pathway: medical management, dietitian-led programs and surgery all have different fee structures, and surgical costs also vary by procedure, private health cover, hospital excess and anaesthetist fees. Some patients explore approved payment or funding pathways, but these should be discussed separately and should not influence whether treatment is clinically appropriate.

Why it matters: The cost copy should reflect the full treatment spectrum and keep credit-style language away from the main medical page.

7. **The draft is roughly 50% over the 1,100-1,400 word target**

> Before any commitment is made, your clinician will go through your individual situation in detail, so that whatever you decide, you'll be deciding with clarity and confidence.

> Delete this sentence. Also cut the repeated "no pressure/no surprises" reassurance in the location and consultation FAQs, shorten the six risk bullets to four, and move procedure detail to internal links so the final body including FAQs lands closer to 1,300-1,400 words.

Why it matters: The canonical body is 1,958 words including FAQs, so repeated reassurance and duplicated assessment language need tightening.

8. **Location naming needs one convention**

> With clinics in Booragoon and Greenfields, appointments and aftercare stay close to home for patients across Perth and the south-west.

> With clinics in Booragoon and Mandurah, appointments and aftercare stay close to home for patients across Perth and the south-west.

Why it matters: Greenfields is the Mandurah suburb housing WA Cardiology Centre, so choose one convention and use it consistently.

9. **Internal links should use cluster anchors**

> Options range from GLP-1 medications and structured very low calorie diets, to dietitian-led nutrition support, exercise guidance, endoscopic procedures, and surgery.

> Options range from non-surgical weight loss, weight-loss medication and dietitian-led support through to gastric sleeve, gastric bypass, gastric band and other bariatric surgery pathways.

Why it matters: Major pathway terms should become sensible internal links rather than remaining unlinked copy.

10. **The medical-management comparison overstates surgical outcomes**

> Surgery physically changes the size or function of your stomach to produce - based on clinical averages - greater, more sustained results for most patients.

> Surgery changes the size or function of your stomach and, for suitable patients, may lead to greater average weight loss than medical management alone; the likely result, risks and long-term commitments vary by person and should be discussed with your clinician.

Why it matters: The existing qualifier helps, but the sentence still needs softer outcome language and clearer individual-variation framing.

## Minor

1. **The hero can be less generic and more credible**

> Whether that is a non-surgical weight loss program or a surgical alternative, we meet you exactly where you are.

> Whether your next step is a non-surgical weight loss program, medication review, dietitian support or a surgical opinion, we help you compare the options clearly before you decide.

Why it matters: The rewrite keeps empathy while giving the reader a clearer sense of the actual pathways.

2. **The spectrum note should be a compact comparison**

> From GLP-1 medications and structured diet programs through to sleeve, bypass, and band procedures, the weight loss treatment spectrum is broader than most people realise.

> Weight loss treatment usually sits on a spectrum: medical management such as a supervised very low calorie diet, allied-health support such as dietitian care and exercise guidance, and surgical options such as gastric sleeve, gastric bypass or gastric band.

Why it matters: A compact comparison is clearer for readers and avoids unnecessary prescription-class emphasis.

3. **The CTA still includes alternate-copy residue**

> You don't need a plan yet. You just need a conversation.

> Not sure where to start? That is exactly the right reason to talk to us.

Why it matters: Keep one finished CTA line and remove leftover alternate-copy scaffolding.
