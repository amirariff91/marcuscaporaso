## Must fix before publish

1. **Medication/BMI eligibility is too absolute**

> Medical weight management options including GLP-1s may be suitable if you have a BMI of 30 or higher.

> Medical weight management options, including GLP-1 medicines where clinically appropriate, may be worth discussing from a BMI of 27 or above when weight-related conditions are present; suitability and surgical Medicare eligibility are assessed separately.

Why it matters: The specific BMI 30+ suitability claim is too direct and should be framed as an assessment conversation, especially where medication suitability and surgical Medicare eligibility are different questions.

2. **The surgery definition creates an unbalanced success claim**

> These are all well established procedures with long track records of weight loss success in Australia.

> These procedures have been used in Australia for many years, but the likely benefits, risks and suitability vary by person and should be discussed with your surgeon.

Why it matters: "Long track records of weight loss success" turns an explanatory definition into a broad promotional outcome claim.

3. **The dietitian FAQ makes an unsupported clinical outcome claim**

> Patients who engage with dietitian support consistently achieve better outcomes, and because our dietitians are in-house, they can work closely with your surgeon throughout your care.

> Dietitian support can help you prepare for treatment, protect your nutrition and build habits that support long-term weight management; because our dietitians are in-house, they can work closely with your surgeon throughout your care.

Why it matters: The rewrite explains the role of dietitian support without making a comparative clinical outcome claim.

   >
   > BMI 30–35: may be considered in select cases, often where diabetes is difficult to manage with medication alone; assessed on an individual basis

   This replaces the implication that BMI 30-35 is a surgical pathway and avoids treating “may be considered” as eligibility.
