# Bariatric Surgery Perth — content review

**Verdict: Rework** · 5 must-fix · 12 should-fix · 4 minor

**Why:** Prescription-medicine references, safety wording, Medicare/private-health certainty, hospital verification and hub-page structure need correction before publication.

**Length:** current body 2,091 words vs brief target 1,200-1,500 words, including FAQs.

## Must fix before publish

1. **TGA prescription-medicine risk: semaglutide is named in public-facing promotional copy**

> Medical weight management: non-surgical weight loss treatment pathways including GLP-1 medications (such as semaglutide) and structured dietitian-led programs.

> Medical weight management: non-surgical weight management pathways, where clinically appropriate, supported by medical assessment, dietitian input and long-term follow-up.

Why it matters: TGA states that "advertising prescription medicines to the public is prohibited" and specifically calls out weight-loss injections in compliance action, so naming semaglutide on a promotional service page is a publish risk: https://www.tga.gov.au/news/media-releases/tga-releases-new-guidance-advertising-restrictions-prescription-medicines.

2. **The FAQ promotes GLP-1 treatment without enough balance [also fix the brief]**

> Medical weight management, including GLP-1 medications and dietitian-led programs, is a genuine and effective option for many people, and our team offers this alongside surgical pathways.

> Surgery is not the only pathway. Some people may be better suited to a non-surgical medical weight management plan, dietitian-led support, or a staged approach before considering surgery; your suitability should be assessed by the clinical team.

Why it matters: This sentence promotes a prescription-medicine class and makes an effectiveness claim without risk context or individual assessment; the writer followed the brief's medical-weight-management direction as instructed, so the brief also needs correction.

3. **The risks section uses an unbalanced "safe" claim [also fix the brief]**

> Every surgical procedure carries risk, though modern keyhole techniques and highly experienced surgeons make bariatric procedures safe for most suitable candidates.

> Every surgical procedure carries risk. For suitable candidates, laparoscopic techniques may reduce some recovery impacts compared with open surgery, but risks still include bleeding, infection, blood clots, anaesthetic complications, leaks, nutritional deficiencies, weight regain and the possibility of revision surgery.

Why it matters: AHPRA's advertising summary says ads should provide accurate risk information and warns against under-representing treatment risk; the writer followed the brief as instructed, so the brief also needs correction: https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Resources/Advertising-hub/Advertising-guidelines-and-other-guidance/Summary-of-the-advertising-requirements.aspx.

4. **Medicare/private-health wording is too definite**

> Medicare rebates apply to most bariatric procedures when eligibility criteria are met, and private health insurance with a gold-tier or equivalent policy covers the hospital and theatre component.

> Medicare rebates and private health insurance benefits may apply to bariatric surgery when the relevant clinical, referral and policy criteria are met. Coverage varies by procedure, fund, waiting periods and level of cover, so OSWA provides an itemised estimate after assessment; current MBS item numbers and eligibility should be verified before publish.

Why it matters: The blocker is the certainty and breadth of "most bariatric procedures" and "covers the hospital and theatre component", because coverage needs item-specific, policy-specific and gap-aware wording.

5. **The opening section keeps a GLP-1 class reference [also fix the brief]**

> Our dedicated patient care team also offers GLP-1 medical weight management, dietetic support, and exercise physiology in-clinic at Booragoon and Greenfields.

> Our dedicated patient care team also offers non-surgical medical weight management, dietetic support, and exercise physiology in-clinic at Booragoon and Greenfields.

Why it matters: If only later GLP-1 references are removed, a prescription-medicine class reference still survives in the opening section; the writer followed the brief's medical-weight-management direction as instructed, so the brief also needs correction.
