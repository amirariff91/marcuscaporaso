### G2 — MBS-inaccurate BMI wording

1. **The summary incorrectly places BMI 30-34 on a qualification pathway**

> As a general guide, surgical assessment is typically available for those with a BMI of ~35, and almost always from 40 and higher. Someone with a BMI of 30 to 34 may qualify where weight-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present and there have been previous attempts at weight loss through diet, exercise, or non-surgical means without lasting success. Please note that these are general clinical indicators only; your surgeon will make a clinical assessment and discuss your options with you when you meet.

> MBS eligibility is assessed individually. The relevant thresholds are BMI 40 or above, or BMI 35–39.9 with at least one major obesity-related condition — always subject to individual clinical assessment. A BMI below 35 does not meet the MBS threshold for these items; your surgeon will assess your wider health picture and discuss any other clinical pathways.

Why it matters: “May qualify” is still self-qualification language, and the MBS rule does not create eligibility below BMI 35.

2. **The BMI 30-34 bullet implies surgery may be appropriate under MBS**

> BMI 30-34 with a weight-related health condition: surgery may be appropriate at this range when conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present, which can strengthen the case for a surgical pathway.

> BMI 30–34 with a weight-related health condition: this range does not meet the MBS threshold for these items. Your surgeon can assess your broader clinical picture and discuss non-MBS options where appropriate; a condition below BMI 35 does not by itself establish eligibility for MBS-funded surgery.

Why it matters: A major comorbidity is relevant to the MBS pathway at BMI 35–39.9; BMI 40 or above is the other threshold limb and does not require a comorbidity, subject to individual clinical assessment.

3. **The BMI 35-39 bullet says other conditions are unnecessary**

> BMI 35-39: most people in this range are eligible for a surgical assessment, with or without other health conditions. A range of procedures may be suitable, and your surgeon will discuss the options most appropriate for your situation.

> BMI 40 or above, or BMI 35–39.9 with at least one major obesity-related condition — always subject to individual clinical assessment. Your surgeon will assess your medical history, risks, goals and aftercare readiness before discussing options.

Why it matters: This is the known G2 error. The BMI 40 or above limb must not be omitted when describing the comorbidity requirement.

4. **The BMI 40+ bullet turns an assessment indicator into an eligibility and effectiveness claim**

> BMI 40+: almost everyone in this range is eligible for surgery and for many patients it represents the most effective long-term pathway available. Your surgeon can confirm which procedure is the right fit.

> BMI 40 or above, or BMI 35–39.9 with at least one major obesity-related condition — always subject to individual clinical assessment. BMI alone does not guarantee eligibility or a particular procedure; your surgeon will discuss whether surgery or another pathway is appropriate for you.

Why it matters: BMI 40+ is an MBS indicator, not a promise of eligibility or comparative effectiveness.

5. **The comorbidity bullet says a condition can strengthen a case regardless of BMI**

> Weight-related health conditions or comorbidities: certain conditions strengthen a surgical case regardless of BMI. These include type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, obstructive sleep apnoea, severe osteoarthritis, PCOS, fatty liver disease (NAFLD/NASH), and severe acid reflux.

> Weight-related health conditions: a major obesity-related comorbidity is relevant to MBS assessment at BMI 35–39.9, but a condition does not create MBS eligibility below BMI 35. Whether a particular condition counts as a major obesity-related comorbidity is assessed individually against the current MBS criteria; your surgeon will assess the full clinical picture.

Why it matters: “Regardless of BMI” conflicts with the MBS threshold and should not appear on an eligibility page.

6. **The BMI 30 FAQ repeats the qualification error**

> **1. My BMI is around 30; am I too low for weight loss surgery?  **
> Not necessarily. A BMI of 30-34 may qualify for a surgical pathway when weight-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present. If surgery is not the right starting point, our medical team can offer meaningful support through medication and dietitian-led care. The only way to know where you stand is a proper assessment.

> **1. My BMI is around 30; am I too low for weight loss surgery?** A BMI of 30-34 does not meet the MBS threshold for these items. Your surgeon can assess your wider clinical picture and discuss non-surgical medical weight management or dietitian-led care where appropriate; an individual assessment is still required.

Why it matters: The FAQ must answer the reader directly without implying that a comorbidity creates an MBS pathway below BMI 35.
