# Am I Eligible for Weight Loss Surgery? — content review

**Verdict: Rework** · 5 must-fix · 12 should-fix · 4 minor

**Why:** Eligibility, mental-health, safety, medication-class, finance and word-count issues need correction before publication.

**Length:** current body 1,908 words vs brief target 1,000-1,300 words, including FAQs.

## Must fix before publish

1. **The BMI 40+ line crosses from assessment criteria into apparent self-qualification**

> BMI 40+: almost everyone in this range is eligible for surgery and for many patients it represents the most effective long-term pathway available.

> BMI 40 and above: people in this range are usually appropriate for a surgical assessment, but suitability still depends on individual clinical review, risks, goals, and whether surgery is the right option among available pathways.

Why it matters: BMI alone should not imply eligibility for surgery, and Australian guidance should be framed as criteria used in assessment.

2. **The mental-health wording is too absolute**

> Psychological wellbeing is part of the assessment process, but it is never a reason to be turned away.

> Psychological wellbeing is part of the assessment process. A mental health history does not automatically rule out surgery, but your surgeon may recommend extra support, further review, or a different timing if that is safer for you.

Why it matters: Active or unstable mental health concerns, eating disorders, substance misuse or inability to follow post-operative care can delay or change suitability, so "never" overpromises.

3. **The preparation step makes an unbalanced "safe and right" claim**

> This stage confirms that surgery is safe and right for you, and prepares you for the changes ahead.

> This stage helps your surgeon assess whether surgery is appropriate for you, identify and explain the risks, and prepare you for the changes ahead.

Why it matters: No surgery should be described as simply "safe", especially on a page that also acknowledges risks.

4. **The cost section has drifted back into the finance cluster**

> For those without private health cover, or where out-of-pocket costs are a concern, financing options such as Zip and TLC are available, and accessing your superannuation for weight loss surgery may be possible in certain circumstances.

> If cost or cover is part of your decision, see our Medicare and private health cover page for a plain-language overview, and ask our team for an itemised estimate at your consultation.

Why it matters: The client moved this page out of the money/finance cluster, so keep only a brief pointer and avoid expanding financing, Zip/TLC or superannuation here.

5. **GLP-1 class references create a prescription-medicine advertising risk [also fix the brief]**

> Our medical team can offer meaningful support through GLP-1 medications and dietitian-led programs.

> Our medical team can offer support through non-surgical medical weight management and dietitian-led programs where clinically appropriate.

Why it matters: TGA treats direct and indirect prescription-class references in public health-service advertising as prohibited, and the writer followed the brief's medical-weight-management direction as instructed, so the brief also needs correction.

## Should fix

1. **The primary keyword is not reinforced in an H2 or first body paragraph**

> This page walks you through who is eligible for weight loss surgery in Perth, what your BMI and health history mean in practice, and what to expect when you meet us.

> If you are asking "am I eligible for weight loss surgery?", this page explains the clinical criteria used in assessment, including BMI, weight-related health conditions, previous weight loss attempts, and what happens when you meet the Obesity Surgery WA team.

Why it matters: The primary keyword appears in the title and H1, but the opening body copy should also use it naturally.

2. **The eligibility summary should frame BMI 30-34 as an assessment pathway**

> Someone with a BMI of 30 to 34 may qualify where weight-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present and there have been previous attempts at weight loss through diet, exercise, or non-surgical means without lasting success.

> Someone with a BMI of 30 to 34 may be considered for surgical assessment when weight-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present, especially after previous non-surgical weight loss attempts have not led to lasting improvement.

Why it matters: "May qualify" is close to self-qualification language, while "may be considered for surgical assessment" is safer and clearer.

3. **The FAQ repeats the same BMI 30-34 qualification issue**

> Not necessarily. A BMI of 30-34 may qualify for a surgical pathway when weight-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present.

> Not necessarily. A BMI of 30-34 may be considered for surgical assessment when weight-related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, or sleep apnoea are present, but your surgeon needs to assess your full health picture before recommending a pathway.

Why it matters: The FAQ is likely to be heavily scanned, so it needs precise assessment-led wording.

4. **The draft includes visible editorial scaffold**

> Not sure where to start? We're here to help.

> The hardest part is often making the first call. Book a consultation and we will help you find out where you stand.

Why it matters: This line is followed by an alternate CTA label, which makes the page look unfinished.

5. **The section heading contains an unresolved alternate**

> What finding the right pathway looks like in practice Alt Where the right assessment can take you

> What the right assessment can help you understand

Why it matters: Unresolved alternate headings are not publishable copy and weaken trust on a medical page.

6. **The second alternate heading has the same problem**

> What to understand before you decide Alt An honest word on what surgery involves

> What to understand before you decide

Why it matters: Leaving alternate labels in the page makes the copy feel unfinished and unreviewed.

7. **Hospital names must be verified with the client before changing either direction**

> Surgeries are performed at [<u>Mount Private Hospital</u>](https://mountprivatehospital.com.au), [<u>The Park Private Hospital</u>](https://www.tpph.com.au/), and [<u>Waikiki Private Hospital</u>](https://waikikiprivate.com.au/).

> Procedures are performed at the Perth hospital locations confirmed by your surgeon, and your team will explain where your procedure and follow-up care would take place during assessment.

Why it matters: The hospital names conflict with the brief, but the draft may reflect newer client information, so verify the correct hospital name(s) with the client before changing either direction.

8. **Internal links are almost entirely missing**

> Your MBBS FRACS surgeon will explain which options are likely to suit you, walk through relevant procedures, and answer your questions.

> Your FRACS bariatric surgeon will explain which options may suit you, including whether gastric sleeve surgery, gastric bypass surgery, non-surgical weight loss support, or another pathway is more appropriate for your situation.

Why it matters: The requested site pages need sensible internal-link anchors for procedure, eligibility, Medicare/private health, non-surgical support, booking, GP referral and FAQs.

9. **The draft misses current Google People-Also-Ask wording checked 10 Jul 2026**

> Is weight loss surgery suitable for you?

> Do I qualify for weight loss surgery in Australia?

Why it matters: The current search result is national and informational, so an H2 should mirror the Australian eligibility intent.

10. **The page needs a general-information disclaimer**

> Your surgeon can confirm which procedure is the right fit.

> This page is general information only and is not personal medical advice. Your surgeon will confirm whether surgery, medication, dietitian-led care, or another pathway is appropriate after an individual assessment.

Why it matters: The brief explicitly asks for a general-information line because the page discusses BMI thresholds.

11. **The cost paragraph repeats itself and inflates the page**

> Before any commitment is made, our team provides a clear, itemised breakdown of what applies to your situation.

> Before you commit, our team will provide an itemised estimate for your situation.

Why it matters: The next sentence repeats the same idea, and the canonical body is 1,908 words against a 1,000-1,300 word target.

12. **The draft does not directly answer who is not eligible**

> You will not leave without options.

> Some people are not suitable for surgery straight away, or may not be suitable at all. Reasons can include a medical condition that makes anaesthetic or surgery too risky, pregnancy, active substance misuse, an untreated eating disorder, or difficulty safely following the required aftercare. Your surgeon will explain this during assessment and discuss non-surgical options where appropriate.

Why it matters: Current Google People-Also-Ask results checked 10 Jul 2026 include "who is not eligible", and the draft should avoid implying everyone can progress to some pathway.

## Minor

1. **A few lines are more persuasive than neutral**

> You are not here because you didn't try hard enough.

> Difficulty achieving lasting weight loss is common in obesity care, and it is one reason a structured medical assessment can be useful.

Why it matters: The rewrite keeps reassurance but makes it more medically grounded.

2. **The benefits section could be clearer about what the consultation answers**

> A clear, personalised answer about whether surgery is right for you, from a surgeon who knows your full picture.

> A surgical eligibility assessment gives you a personalised answer based on BMI, weight-related health conditions, previous weight loss attempts, medical risk, readiness for aftercare, and your goals.

Why it matters: Specific criteria make the answer more useful for readers and search snippets.

3. **A full FAQ answer can be cut without losing brief adherence**

> Your initial consultation runs for 30-60 minutes and is a relaxed, honest conversation about your weight history, health, and goals.

> At your first appointment, your surgeon reviews your weight history, health conditions, goals, and questions, then explains which pathways may be suitable.

Why it matters: This repeats the orientation consultation section almost word-for-word and can be combined or shortened to help meet the target length.

4. **The CTA should link directly to booking**

> You don't need a plan yet. You just need a conversation.

> Book a consultation to find out where you stand.

Why it matters: The brief's purpose is activation toward consultation, and the CTA should avoid another alternate line.
