# Super Access for Weight Loss Surgery — content review

**Verdict: Rework** · 5 blocking · 7 should-fix

**Why:** The educational core is strong, but compassionate release is framed too persuasively in places. The page also needs clearer financial boundaries, verified hospital information, the required keyword H2, removal of editorial scaffolding and a substantial cut to meet the brief.

## Must fix before publish

1. **Severity: Blocking — The page frames early-release super as a way to obtain surgery sooner**

> For many Perth patients, it's still the difference between having surgery this year and waiting several more.

> **Why some Perth patients choose to access super**
>
> - It can remove the cost barrier and let you have surgery sooner, rather than waiting years to save the gap payment.
>
> - Acting earlier may help address health conditions such as type 2 diabetes or high blood pressure before they progress further.
>
> - It uses money that already belongs to you, so there's no interest charge as there would be with a loan.
>
> - The application is lodged directly with the ATO through myGov, so you stay in control of the process and the information you provide.
>
> - It can be combined with private health cover or an external payment plan to cover the full cost of your procedure and/or hospital stay.

Why it matters: This reads as an inducement to withdraw retirement savings and uses urgency, comparative finance and implied health-outcome benefits.

**Writer edit:** Replace the quoted sentence and the full benefits section with:

> ## Considering compassionate release
>
> Some patients consider early release when they cannot meet a documented treatment expense from their available resources. The ATO applies strict criteria and assesses each application individually; approval is not automatic. Early release reduces retirement savings and may have tax consequences, so consider the personal implications with a licensed financial adviser. OSWA's financial coordinator can explain the clinic estimate and administrative process, but cannot provide financial advice or influence the ATO's decision.

**Brief-owner note (OSWA, not the writer):** The brief itself mandates this copy — its Benefits section instructs the five benefit bullets ("remove the cost barrier and enable surgery sooner", "no interest charges", "can be combined with private health insurance or a payment plan") and its core section mandates "for many patients it is still the difference between having surgery now versus waiting years". The writer followed instructions; rewrite those brief sections to the compassionate-release framing above.

> Once you're clinically suitable for surgery, here's what the ATO looks for in a compassionate release application:

Why it matters: One specialist’s certification should not appear to establish eligibility, and clinical suitability for surgery is a separate question from compassionate-release approval.

**Writer edit:** Replace both quoted passages with:

> Clinical suitability for surgery and eligibility for compassionate release are separate assessments. Bariatric surgery, including gastric sleeve, gastric bypass and mini gastric bypass, is not pre-approved. The ATO applies strict criteria, including whether treatment is needed for a qualifying serious medical condition, is not readily available through the public system, and cannot be paid for another way. Two registered medical practitioners must support the application, including at least one relevant specialist. The ATO decides each application on its own facts.
