### What is weight loss medication, and how does it actually work?

- ~3 paragraphs (200–280 words total for this section)
- **Para 1:** Explain GLP-1 medications as a class. Refer to them by active ingredient (semaglutide, tirzepatide), NOT by brand name, in published body copy — see the TGA note below. Explain simply how GLP-1 medications work — they mimic a gut hormone that signals fullness, so the reader feels satisfied sooner and thinks about food less.
- **Para 2:** Introduce phentermine (use the generic name, not the brand) as an older, different type of appetite suppressant that has been used in Australia for many years. Note it is usually prescribed for shorter periods and is not right for everyone. It needs proper medical assessment.
- **Para 3:** Land the key point: medication alone is not the whole answer. It works best alongside eating, movement, and sleep changes. This is why OSW uses physicians and dietitians working together, not a script-and-send model. The comparison note to weave in here (or as a callout): medication is less invasive and reversible than surgery, but weight loss is typically smaller and may return if the medication is stopped without a long-term plan.

### Who is a good candidate for weight loss medication?

- **BMI note:** Generally considered for adults with a BMI of 30+, or a BMI of 27+ when weight-related health conditions are present (e.g., type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnoea).
- **Bullet points to address — the reader should see themselves in at least one of these:**
  - Has tried diet and lifestyle changes without reaching or maintaining a healthy weight
  - Has weight affecting their health, joints, sleep, or daily quality of life
  - Wants a non-surgical option, or is not yet ready to consider surgery
  - Is willing to attend regular reviews — medication is monitored, not set-and-forget

Keep the tone non-judgemental. This is a list of real situations, not a clinical checklist.

### How it works — the OSW medication program (6 steps)

Write one to two sentences of engaging, practical guidance per step. Not bullet points — give each step a sense of what the reader will actually experience.

1. **GP Referral** — Ask your GP for a referral. This allows the team to review your full health picture and access Medicare rebates on eligible consultations.
2. **Orientation Consultation** — A conversation about your weight history, your health, and your goals. The team explains which medication options may suit you and which would not.
3. **Pre-treatment Assessment** — A physician reviews your medical history, current medications and any existing conditions to confirm the chosen medication is safe for you. Baseline blood tests if needed.
4. **Starting Medication** — A low starting dose, increased gradually. This slow build reduces early side effects like nausea and helps the body adjust.
5. **Dietitian Support** — A dietitian helps you eat well on the medication, protect muscle mass, and build habits that keep working even after treatment ends.
6. **Ongoing Review** — Regular check-ins to review progress, adjust the dose, manage side effects, and plan for maintaining results long term.

### What weight loss medication can do for you (5 benefits)

Write each benefit as a plain, honest statement — not marketing language. Cover:

1. Clinical average weight loss of around 10–15% of body weight on GLP-1 medication (individual results vary)
2. Reduced hunger and fewer food cravings, making healthy eating more achievable
3. Improved blood sugar control — particularly relevant for people with or at risk of type 2 diabetes
4. A non-surgical option that can be started sooner, with no hospital stay or operation recovery
5. Care from a full specialist team (physicians and dietitians together) — not a tablet on its own

### Being honest about the side effects and limits

- **Intro paragraph tone:** Calm and matter-of-fact. Acknowledge that every effective medication carries some risk. Most side effects are manageable and settle with time. The team starts low and goes slow, and they are upfront that medication suits some people better than others.
- **Risk bullets to address:**
  - GLP-1 medications commonly cause nausea, constipation, or reflux — especially in the first few weeks
  - Phentermine can raise heart rate and blood pressure, and may cause restlessness or trouble sleeping; it is not suitable for some heart conditions
  - Weight tends to return if medication is stopped without a long-term eating and activity plan
  - Rapid weight loss can cause some muscle loss and, for some people, looser skin — dietitian support and resistance exercise help manage this

Do not downplay these. Honest acknowledgement of risk builds trust.

---

### Cost paragraph

Cover these points in one clear, confident paragraph (no dot points):
- Costs depend on which medication is prescribed, the dose, and treatment duration — so a specific figure is discussed openly at consultation, not quoted as a single number
- Physician consultations attract the higher referred Medicare rebate with a valid GP referral; a lower rebate applies without one
- Most GLP-1 weight loss medications are currently an out-of-pocket cost (not PBS-subsidised for weight loss specifically) and pharmacy prices can change
- Private health cover does not usually pay for the medication itself, though it may contribute to related care
- The team will always give a clear picture of expected costs before the patient commits to anything

## Tone and compliance notes

- **Medical advertising:** No guarantees. Use phrases like "clinical averages", "results vary", "your specialist will advise", "individual results differ".
- **AHPRA/TGA:** No testimonials, no before/after claims, no promised outcomes.
- **Voice:** Plain-language, empathetic, direct. Avoid hype and jargon. Write like a knowledgeable friend, not a brochure.
- **Reader:** A Perth adult who has struggled with weight and is doing careful research. Likely anxious, possibly embarrassed about past attempts. Wants to be treated as an intelligent adult. May have seen GLP-1 medications in the news and wants to understand them properly before committing to anything.
- **Medication names:** Prescription-only medicines must not be advertised to the public. In published body copy, refer to medications by active ingredient/class (semaglutide, tirzepatide, phentermine, GLP-1) and AVOID brand names and dose/availability/approval specifics; do not make prescribing claims.
