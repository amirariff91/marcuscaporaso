## Page purpose
This page walks someone from first thought ("surgery might be an option for me") through to the months after the operation: referral, consultation and assessment, surgery day, hospital recovery, and ongoing aftercare to support long-term nutrition, monitor progress and help identify or manage regain. It needs to remove the fear of the unknown, be completely honest that this is a stepwise process with commitments at every stage, and hand the reader to the right next page depending on where they are in the pathway.

### SERP Landscape (AU)
Two honest observations shape how this page should be judged:

1. **The "what to expect" family has no commercial clinic competition.** For journey/recovery-type queries the top of the SERP is high-authority hospital and health portals — Johns Hopkins Medicine, healthdirect.gov.au, Alberta Health, UCSF Health, Mayo Clinic (DR 80–92) — plus zero-DR Australian clinic blog posts (e.g. centralcoastsurgery.com.au "gastric sleeve recovery time", DR 25). No Australian bariatric clinic runs a dedicated full-pathway journey page. Nobody is competing for this intent with local, practice-specific content — which is precisely the gap: the portals describe the procedure generically and never mention referral logistics, Medicare, Perth hospitals or aftercare appointments.

A clear pathway page is a differentiator none of them offers, and it will earn internal links from every existing OSWA content page.

### Content Gap Opportunity
Three clear gaps. (1) **Nobody owns the end-to-end pathway.** Ranking pages cover either the procedure (local clinics) or recovery (portals). A single, honest Referral → Assessment → Surgery → Aftercare narrative does not exist in the local SERP and is the highest-value content this site alone can credibly write, because it is the only practice that can describe its own appointments, its own hospitals and its own aftercare schedule. (2) **Expectation-setting for each stage, not just the surgery.** Competitor pages tell patients what the operation involves but not what happens in the weeks before (dietitian visits, physician review, fund paperwork) or the months after (follow-up cadence, diet transitions). (3) **The "it's a commitment" honesty angle.** Every competitor page frames surgery as the finish line; the credible move — and the one AHPRA-compliant framing demands — is to make clear the surgery is one step in a longer process, with aftercare to support long-term nutrition, monitor progress and help identify or manage regain. That honesty is what this site's other pages already lead with, and it should anchor this page too.
