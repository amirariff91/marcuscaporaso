## Section guidance

### Hero
- **Headline direction:** Address the question directly and honestly. The reader typed "gastric sleeve cost Perth" because they want a real answer. Don't dodge it; tell them you'll explain how the cost is built and what shapes it.
- **Subheadline:** Acknowledge that working out the real cost is genuinely confusing when multiple providers bill separately. Commit to explaining the components, what Medicare and private health cover, and the questions to ask before committing. Practical, calm tone — not a sales pitch.

### Stats bar (4 stats)
These establish credibility and set expectations before the reader hits the detail. Write them as factual anchors:
1. **Separate fee types / 4+** — surgeon, hospital, anaesthetist, physician (briefly define what each covers)
2. **Medicare item / 31575** — surgical rebate applies when the patient meets MBS eligibility (BMI 40+ or 35+ with comorbidities); item number is publicly available MBS information
3. **Private cover / Required** — appropriate hospital cover tier needed; note many policies require a higher tier for bariatric procedures
4. **Written estimate / Provided** — before any decision, no obligation

### Why is gastric sleeve pricing in Perth hard to pin down? (3 paragraphs)

**Paragraph 1 — No single price tag**
Explain that there is no one figure because the total is made up of several separate fees billed by different providers. Name the main components: surgeon's fee, anaesthetist's fee, hospital (facility) fee, physician and dietitian fees for assessment and follow-up. Two patients can pay different amounts for the same operation — this is normal in Australian private surgery.

**Paragraph 2 — What drives the variation**
The factors that move the number: the patient's private health insurance policy and what it covers, the level of hospital cover held, whether the waiting period has been served, and the individual's clinical needs. Keep this factual and neutral — don't scare or oversell.

**Paragraph 3 — The written estimate commitment**
Explain that a written, itemised estimate is provided before any decision is made. This is how the practice operates — no surprises. The estimate covers every fee line so the patient can plan accurately.

### Comparison note (inline callout)
Short practical note: a gastric sleeve and a gastric bypass carry different fee structures and different Medicare item numbers. Readers comparing procedures should use like-for-like estimates, not headline figures from different procedures or practices.

### Eligibility and Medicare suitability
Frame as a clinical and financial checklist. Cover:
- Medicare rebates and the surgical item number (31575) apply only when defined clinical criteria are met — typically BMI 40+, or 35+ with comorbidities; eligibility is assessed by the surgeon, not assumed
- Private health funds require an appropriate hospital cover tier that explicitly includes bariatric (weight loss) surgery; many require a higher tier policy; many apply a 12-month waiting period
- GP referral and pre-operative assessments confirm clinical eligibility before surgery is scheduled
- Eligibility is a clinical decision made with the surgeon, not simply a matter of ability to pay

### How it works (6 steps)
Write each step as a clear numbered action covering both the clinical pathway and where costs arise at each stage.

1. **GP Referral** — Patient is referred to the practice; referral supports Medicare eligibility and allows the team to review history before the first meeting
2. **Orientation Consultation** — Patient meets the team, discusses whether a sleeve is appropriate; receives a written, itemised cost estimate covering surgeon, anaesthetist and hospital fees at this stage
3. **Pre-operative Assessment** — Physicians and dietitians assess health; team confirms private health cover, waiting period status and whether super release may be an option
4. **Surgery** — Keyhole procedure performed at Mount Private Hospital or Waikiki Private Hospital; surgeon and anaesthetist fees relate to this stage
5. **Hospital Recovery** — Typically around two nights; hospital facility fee covers room, theatre and nursing during admission
6. **Ongoing Support** — Dietitian and physician follow-up is part of long-term care; some reviews attract Medicare rebates; the team explains what to expect

### What you get for the cost (benefits)
Write as a bulleted list, five points. These should describe the actual value of the care model, not make outcome promises.
- A coordinated care team — specialist bariatric surgeons, physicians and specialist dietitians — not surgery as a standalone event
- Keyhole (laparoscopic) surgery at accredited private hospitals (Mount Private Hospital and Waikiki Private Hospital)
- A clear, itemised written estimate provided upfront so the patient can plan and compare with confidence
- Structured pre-operative assessment and long-term follow-up built into the care pathway, not billed as unexpected extras
- Clinical averages show patients lose approximately 60–70% of excess weight in the first one to two years — include a clear caveat that results vary between individuals and this is not a promise

### Honest talk about cost, gaps and value (risks / pitfalls)
**Tone:** Frank and practical. The reader has a legitimate financial concern; this section respects that by naming the actual risks around cost. Not scary — just honest.

**Opening paragraph:** Identify the most common source of financial surprise — unexpected out-of-pocket gaps — and explain they usually arise from not checking private health cover, being unaware of waiting periods, or comparing only the surgeon's fee while overlooking the hospital and anaesthetist. Framing: knowing these in advance is the protection.

**Risk bullet points (4 items):**
- An out-of-pocket gap may remain after Medicare rebates and private health benefits are applied
- Inadequate or recently upgraded hospital cover can mean a waiting period applies or limited benefit is paid
- Additional fees can arise if complications or extra investigations are needed — this is true of any surgery
- Choosing the cheapest quote without the same care inclusions can cost more overall when follow-up is excluded

### Cost paragraph
This is the most important section on the page for conversion — it must be specific enough to be useful but compliant. Write:
- For privately insured patients with appropriate cover: out-of-pocket after Medicare and fund rebates **typically ranges from $2,000 to $8,000**, depending on specific policy, excess and surgeon fees
- For self-funded or inadequately covered patients: total cost **typically $15,000 to $30,000 or more**
- Clearly state these are approximate ranges; the figure that matters is the personalised written estimate
- Explain that patients without suitable cover or within a waiting period may be eligible to apply for early super release on compassionate/medical grounds
- Mention third-party payment plans as an option
- State the estimate is walked through line by line at the orientation consultation — no surprises is the practice standard

Do not present these figures as quotes. Always frame as approximate planning ranges.

### Outcomes note
Clinical averages, not promises. Patients commonly lose around 60–70% of excess weight in the first one to two years following a gastric sleeve; many see improvement in associated conditions (type 2 diabetes, sleep apnoea, high blood pressure). Lead with the caveat: these are averages, not guarantees. Individual outcomes depend on starting health, dietary and lifestyle commitment, and follow-up. The long-term value of surgery comes from ongoing support, not the operation alone.

### FAQs
Write each answer in plain, direct language. These are the questions a cost-focused reader needs answered before they will pick up the phone.

1. How much does a gastric sleeve cost in Perth? (Range: $2,000–$8,000 OOP for well-insured patients; $15,000–$30,000+ self-funded; written estimate provided; MBS item 31575 applies)
2. Why are there so many separate fees? (Surgeon, anaesthetist, hospital each bill independently; dietitian and physician assessment also billed separately; the practice brings these together in one estimate)
3. Does Medicare cover gastric sleeve surgery? (When the procedure meets the Medicare item criteria, a rebate applies on surgical and anaesthetist fees; the GP referral separately sets the higher referred rate on consultations; Medicare does not cover the full cost; a private contribution typically remains)
4. Will my private health insurance pay for it? (Requires appropriate hospital cover including bariatric surgery item numbers; most funds apply a 12-month waiting period; the practice helps confirm what the fund will contribute before the patient commits)
5. Can I use my superannuation to pay? (Some patients apply on compassionate/medical grounds; assessed by ATO and super fund, not the practice; the practice can explain the process and provide supporting clinical information)
6. Are there payment plans available? (Some patients use third-party medical payment plans; terms and interest are between patient and provider; the practice can point to available options)
7. How long will I need off work? (Approximately two weeks; desk-based workers often return sooner; surgeon advises based on individual role and recovery)
8. Will I have loose skin, and can I eat normally afterwards? (Loose skin varies; discuss with surgeon. After recovery and reintroduction guided by dietitian, most people eat a wide range of healthy foods in smaller portions)

## Tone and compliance notes
- No fixed price quotes — always describe as approximate planning ranges and refer to the written estimate
- MBS item 31575 may be mentioned factually; eligibility must always be described as clinically assessed and not assumed
- Medicare eligibility criteria (BMI thresholds) should be stated as general clinical guidance, not guarantees of coverage
- No outcome promises — all weight loss figures must be framed as clinical averages with explicit variance caveat
- Super release: describe the process accurately; do not imply it is straightforward or automatic
- AHPRA/TGA compliance: no testimonials, no "guaranteed results", no before/after framing
- Voice: transparent, practical, calm — the reader is worried about money; meet them there and reduce the anxiety through clarity
