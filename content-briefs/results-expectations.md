## Page purpose
This page answers the quiet-but-universal question every weight loss surgery patient has and often won't say out loud: *what are my results actually going to be?* It needs to be the most honest results-and-expectations page a Perth patient can find — covering the realistic pattern of weight loss over time, what is realistic versus what is social-media fiction, the day-to-day recovery and body changes, and the honest limitations (some patients lose less than average, some regain, some need revision). The page's credibility comes from expectation-setting, not from promising outcomes. It is squarely an AHPRA-compliance-sensitive page: no outcome or superiority claims, no percentages asserted as our own, no before/after framing.

### What "results" actually measure
- ~2 paragraphs
- Para 1: Explain that surgeons measure results as a percentage of *excess* weight (the weight above a healthy range), not total body weight — and that this is why published averages sound smaller than patients expect in kilograms. Define excess weight loss (EWL) and excess body weight in plain language.
- Para 2: Explain why averages mislead: a range means some patients do better and some do worse, and factors like starting weight, age, activity and the pattern of long-term follow-up may be associated with different trajectories. Set up the reader psychologically for the range rather than a promise.

### The realistic timeline (first year and beyond)
- The core section of the page. Use a short intro plus a 4-row table (FS target: this table is the featured-snippet play for "gastric sleeve weight loss timeline").
- Timeline rows: (1) First 2 weeks — healing, fluids, low energy, hospital-to-home transition. (2) Months 1–3 — steepest loss phase, portion sizes very small, food tolerances unsettled, energy fluctuating. (3) Months 4–6 — loss continues but slows, diet reintroduces more variety, dietitian reviews protein and hydration. (4) Months 7–12 and beyond — loss often slows; plateaus may occur, but trajectories vary and may remain erratic through 12–18 months; focus shifts to maintenance, muscle preservation, supplements and follow-up.
- **Every quantitative cell in this table must be qualitative or cited-published only.** No practice percentage. Where a writer would naturally reach for a figure, insert `<!-- GATED: Wendy to confirm cited range -->` and write around it.
- Close the section with one sentence noting surgery is performed at Mount Private Hospital and Waikiki Private Hospital, with follow-up reviews at our Booragoon and Subiaco / West Leederville consulting rooms.

### Recovery: what the body actually goes through
- ~150–200 words as prose, or 5–6 tight bullets
- Cover honestly: incision/keyhole healing and typical restriction on lifting; fatigue in the early weeks; the constipation and dehydration trap; hair shedding, which is often temporary but varies in timing and regrowth (explain why it can happen and advise clinical review if it persists); food intolerance variability (some patients tolerate foods differently for months).
- Frame: "this is common, this is why we keep seeing you, tell us if it doesn't settle" — never dismissive, never alarming.

### When results are less than expected (the honest section)
- ~2–3 paragraphs. This is the differentiated section — no AU competitor covers it.
- Para 1: Name it directly: some patients lose less than the published average, and some regain a proportion of lost weight in later years. Published long-term studies document both. This is not a failure of the patient by definition — but it does need attention.
- Para 2: Describe the concrete purpose of follow-up: monitoring nutrition, protein and hydration, reviewing movement and dietitian-guided habits, and catching regain early. Do not imply that attendance guarantees a better outcome; biological, procedural, medical and behavioural factors all contribute to outcomes.
- Para 3: The next step — if weight loss has stalled well short of expectations or regain is significant, a clinical review can assess causes and, where appropriate, discuss revision options. Link internally to revision surgery (see Internal Links). Be matter-of-fact, not defeatist.

### Risks and limitations
- Short paragraph plus 4–5 bullets, honest and proportionate. This page is about expectations, so the risk framing here is "everything has trade-offs" rather than a full surgical-risk list — link to the procedure pages for full risk content.
- Bullets: results differ between individuals and no outcome can be guaranteed; the sleeve leaves the stomach permanently smaller (some foods never feel the same again — answers a PAA question); some patients experience new acid reflux or dumping-type symptoms after eating; long-term supplement needs are part of the deal, not optional; biological, procedural, medical and behavioural factors all contribute to outcomes, and lifestyle habits are one part of that picture.

- One short paragraph. Point readers who now understand results to the practical pages: how Medicare and private health cover apply, and what surgery costs in Perth — internal links only, no cost figures on this page.
