## Page purpose
This page introduces Obesity Surgery WA's accredited practising dietitian (APD) team and explains why dietetic care is a core part of the surgical journey — before the operation, through recovery, and in the years after. It should reassure anxious readers ("will I ever eat normally again?") and explain the practice's confirmed allied-health delivery model rather than assume that care is a one-off nutrition appointment. The page is the allied-health bridge between /surgeons and the procedure pages. <!-- GATED: confirm in-house allied-health operating model with Wendy -->

### Honest expectations section (risks / limitations)
Calm and frank, in the house style for risk content. Opening paragraph: no diet plan or surgical procedure guarantees a particular weight-loss result; the dietitian's role is to give the patient the best-supported plan and to stay involved when things go off track. Bullets to address:
- Surgery changes the stomach, not habits — without ongoing dietary change, weight can return
- Some patients experience side effects the dietitian helps manage: dumping syndrome symptoms, food intolerances, or difficulty tolerating certain foods
- Nutritional deficiencies can develop after some procedures; long-term monitoring is part of care, not an optional extra
- Eating changes are significant, especially in the first months; patients should be prepared for the staged plan
- General point: suitability and specifics are always confirmed by the consulting team — this page explains the role, it does not assess anyone
