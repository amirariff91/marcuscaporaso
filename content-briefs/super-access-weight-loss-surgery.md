### What does it mean to use super for weight loss surgery?
Write ~3 paragraphs. This is the educational core of the page — the reader needs a clear mental model before any detail lands.

1. **The normal rules and the exception:** Explain that super is normally locked until retirement or preservation age, but the ATO can approve early release on compassionate grounds — and medical treatment that is not readily available through the public system is one of those grounds.
2. **What qualifies and the ATO's role:** Bariatric surgery (gastric sleeve, bypass, mini bypass) can fall within the medical-treatment category when recommended for a serious condition like severe obesity, type 2 diabetes or sleep apnoea. Make clear: the ATO assesses each application on its own facts. No procedure is pre-approved.
3. **The honest framing:** This is not a loan and not free money — it is retirement savings brought forward, and it is taxed. For many patients it is still the difference between having surgery now versus waiting years, but it is a decision that deserves care and ideally independent financial advice.

---

### Eligibility
- **BMI note:** There is no BMI threshold for accessing super itself. Eligibility is decided by the ATO based on medical and financial circumstances, not weight.
- **Criteria to address:**
  1. Treating doctors must confirm in writing that the surgery is needed to treat a serious medical condition (severe obesity and its complications).
  2. Two registered medical practitioners must support the application; at least one must be a specialist in the relevant field.
  3. The applicant generally needs to show they cannot pay for the treatment without accessing super — i.e., they don't have savings or another way to fund the gap.
  4. Not all super funds release money the same way — even after ATO approval, some funds have their own rules and processing steps, so the patient should contact their fund early.

---

### How it works (steps)
Six steps. Each should be 2–3 sentences. The reader should come away with a clear, actionable sequence — not a vague sense that it's complicated.

1. **Get your GP referral and surgical plan** — Start with a GP referral to OSWA. At the consultation, the team confirms clinical suitability and provides a cost estimate the patient can attach to the ATO application.
2. **Obtain supporting medical reports** — The ATO requires reports from two registered medical practitioners (one a specialist). OSWA's team can help prepare the documentation explaining why surgery is needed to treat the patient's condition.
3. **Lodge your application with the ATO** — Apply directly through myGov linked to the ATO. The form covers the treatment, cost, medical practitioners and why the expense can't be met another way. Online lodgement is typically faster than paper.
4. **Wait for the ATO decision** — The ATO reviews the application and, if approved, issues a determination letter and notifies the super fund of the approved amount. A complete application is typically decided within a few weeks.
5. **Your super fund releases the funds** — The fund processes payment under its own rules. Tax is withheld at this stage. Money is paid to the patient (or sometimes directly to the provider); the patient then settles surgery costs.
6. **Proceed to surgery and ongoing support** — With funding confirmed, the team schedules pre-operative assessment and surgery at SJOG Murdoch or Subiaco, followed by the Perth-based dietitian and aftercare program.

### Benefits
Keep each benefit honest and grounded. This section should not read like a sales pitch for using super — it should read like an honest articulation of why patients do it.

1. It can remove the cost barrier and enable surgery sooner rather than waiting years to save a lump sum.
2. Acting earlier may address health conditions (type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnoea) before they progress further.
3. It uses money that already belongs to the patient — no interest charges as there would be with a loan.
4. The application is lodged directly with the ATO through myGov, so the patient controls the process and the information provided.
5. It can be combined with private health insurance or a payment plan to cover the full cost of the procedure and hospital stay.

### Risks / watch-out section
- **Intro tone:** Genuinely cautionary but not alarmist. The reader should feel the clinic is being honest with them, not trying to talk them out of a legitimate option. The key message is: this is a real trade-off that deserves real consideration.
- **Risk items to cover:**
  1. Released super is taxed — the amount received will be less than the gross amount withdrawn from the account.
  2. Withdrawing now reduces the retirement balance and the compounding growth it would have earned over time.
  3. ATO approval is not automatic — applications that do not meet the compassionate-release criteria are declined.
  4. Processing takes time — factor in several weeks between lodging and having funds available.

Include a clear, unambiguous recommendation to speak with a licensed financial adviser before applying.

## Tone and compliance notes
- No guarantees on ATO approval — be explicit that the ATO assesses each application on its own facts and approval is not automatic.
- No guarantees on financial outcomes — tax rates and retirement impact vary by individual; always direct the reader to a licensed financial adviser for personalised modelling.
- Be clear that OSWA's role is clinical, not financial advisory — the clinic helps with medical documentation but cannot influence ATO decisions or advise on the retirement trade-off.
- Voice: plain-language, honest, treats the reader as an adult capable of making a considered decision when given accurate information.
- Reader profile: cost-anxious patient who has heard super release is possible and wants to know if it applies to them and how to do it. They may be sceptical it is legitimate, or may be excited about it without fully understanding the trade-offs. The page needs to serve both.
