### What is excess skin removal after weight loss?
- ~3 paragraphs.
- Para 1: After significant/major weight loss the skin does not always shrink back; many are left with redundant skin, most commonly a pannus over the lower abdomen. Acknowledge that for some this is purely cosmetic and they live with it; for others it causes medical/functional problems.
- Para 2: Define panniculectomy — surgical removal of the overhanging fold of redundant skin and underlying tissue. Reconstructive, considered for documented problems (recurrent infections, rashes/intertrigo, hygiene difficulty, skin breakdown/ulceration, impaired movement). Here is where "abdominoplasty" may appear ONCE as a related clinical term, explicitly in its reconstructive sense.
- Para 3: State plainly — at this practice the procedure is assessed and offered strictly on medical grounds to treat a functional problem; it is NOT an appearance procedure; eligibility decided individually.

### Comparison / approach note
Short bridging paragraph on weight stability: only considered once weight stable for a sustained period (~6–12 months); operating earlier risks further redundancy; stability is part of medical assessment, not an arbitrary wait.

### Eligibility
- BMI note: No single number determines suitability — what matters is whether redundant skin is causing documented functional/medical problems, weight stability, and fitness for surgery; assessed individually.
- Bullets:
  - Significant overhanging fold of redundant skin (pannus) after major weight loss
  - Pannus causing documented medical problems (recurrent infections, rashes/intertrigo, skin breakdown/ulceration, hygiene difficulty)
  - Redundant skin interfering with movement/daily function
  - Weight stable for a sustained period (~6–12 months)
  - Medically fit for major surgery (surgeon + anaesthetist assess)
  - Medicare/private fund support applies ONLY to medically-justified cases meeting criteria; never automatic; assessed individually
  - If not medically indicated or not in the patient's best interest, the surgeon will say so honestly

### How it works (6 steps)
1. **GP Referral** — referral to the practice, ideally noting the skin problems; gives the higher referred Medicare rebate on consultations; first step to assessment.
2. **Medical Assessment** — surgeon examines redundant skin, reviews history of infections/rashes/breakdown and prior treatment; establishes whether there is a genuine documented functional problem.
3. **Confirming Weight Stability** — confirm weight stable for a sustained period (~6–12 months) before recommending surgery.
4. **Eligibility and Funding Review** — review whether the case meets the medical criteria allowing Medicare/private fund support (relevant MBS panniculectomy criteria); assessed individually, never assumed. (No finance/super inducement language.)
5. **Surgery** — if medically indicated, performed at SJOG Murdoch or Subiaco; major operation; surgeon explains approach, expected scarring, hospital stay.
6. **Recovery and Follow-Up** — recovery takes time and careful wound care; follow-up to monitor healing, manage drains, watch for seroma/wound-healing problems.

### Benefits (5 bullets) — framed as what the procedure AIMS to address
- Removal of a heavy overhanging fold of redundant skin causing documented medical problems
- Aims to reduce recurrent skin infections, rashes and intertrigo in the folds
- May improve ability to keep the area clean and dry (hygiene)
- Aims to relieve skin breakdown, ulceration or chafing from the weight/friction of the skin
- May improve movement/daily function where the pannus has been limiting (results vary; surgeon advises)

### Risks / honest section (prominent — mandatory)
- Intro paragraph: direct — this is major surgery with real risks; large incision, significant tissue removal, long scar, lengthy recovery; given out of respect not to alarm; most recover well; surgeon discusses individual risk.
- Risk bullets:
  - Significant and permanent scarring (large incision)
  - Seroma (fluid collection), common, may need draining
  - Wound-healing problems and wound breakdown, may take time to settle
  - Bleeding and infection
  - Blood clots (legs/lungs) — prevention and early movement important
  - Anaesthetic risks
  - Possible need for further surgery/revision; no guaranteed result; outcomes vary

### Cost paragraph
Cover: offered only where medically indicated, so total depends on whether the case meets relevant Medicare/private fund criteria, hospital cover level, surgeon/anaesthetist fees, hospital stay. Medicare/fund support applies only to medically-justified cases meeting criteria and is individually assessed, never automatic. Provide a clear itemised written estimate AFTER assessment once medical indication is established. **Do NOT introduce superannuation early-release, finance or payment-plan promotion in the copy.**

### Outcomes note
Honest paragraph: the procedure aims to relieve functional/skin problems from the pannus; many find those problems improve; reconstructive with a medical purpose, NOT an appearance procedure; no promises about appearance; results vary; healing takes time; scarring permanent; if not medically indicated the surgeon will say so; surgeon advises what is realistic.
