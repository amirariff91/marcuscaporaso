### What payment options are available for bariatric surgery?
Write ~3 paragraphs covering each funding pathway in turn. Guidance per paragraph:

1. **Private health + Medicare:** Explain that this is the most common route. Cover how Medicare contributes to eligible surgeon and anaesthetist fees, while private hospital cover handles theatre and hospital costs. Be clear the amount left for the patient depends on their specific policy — no promises.
2. **Third-party medical finance:** Explain what a payment plan is (spreading cost over time), but be explicit that these are arranged through independent providers, not OSWA directly. Terms, rates and approval sit with the provider, not the clinic.
3. **Superannuation / no single answer:** Briefly introduce super early release and no-gap as additional possibilities. Land on the key message: because every situation is different, a consultation with a written estimate is the only reliable way to know your real cost.

---

### Eligibility
- **BMI note:** Funding options are generally relevant for patients with a BMI of 35+, or BMI 30+ with related health conditions, who are clinically suitable for surgery.
- **Criteria to address (one point per item, plain language):**
  1. Private health cover must specifically include bariatric surgery and have no active waiting period — the reader should check their policy, not assume.
  2. Medical finance: approval, interest and repayment terms are entirely the provider's decision, not OSWA's.
  3. Superannuation early release is decided by the ATO, not OSWA — the clinic cannot influence that outcome.
  4. OSWA provides a clear, itemised written estimate before surgery so the patient can plan without surprises.

### How it works (steps)
Six steps — keep each step concise (2–3 sentences). Focus on what the patient does and what they receive at each stage.

1. **GP Referral** — Obtaining a valid referral so Medicare can contribute to eligible specialist and surgical fees.
2. **Orientation Consultation** — Surgery suitability is assessed; costs, health fund options and funding pathways are discussed openly.
3. **Written Estimate** — Patient receives an itemised breakdown: surgeon, anaesthetist, hospital and expected gap. No surprises.
4. **Arrange Funding** — Patient confirms health fund cover, applies to a finance provider if needed, or explores super release.
5. **Confirm and Schedule** — Once funding is settled and pre-op checks are done, surgery is booked at SJOG Murdoch or Subiaco.
6. **Ongoing Support** — Dietitian and clinical team support continue post-surgery. Follow-up care is part of the program, not an add-on.

### Benefits
Write each benefit as a short, plainly stated value (1–2 sentences). Avoid hype. These should feel honest and useful.

1. Spreading cost over time can make surgery accessible sooner rather than requiring years of saving.
2. Using existing private hospital cover often significantly reduces out-of-pocket costs.
3. A written, itemised estimate up front means the patient knows what they are committing to before they decide.
4. Combining pathways — for example, health fund cover plus finance for the gap — can lower the immediate lump sum needed.
5. The team explains each option clearly so decisions are informed, not rushed.

### Risks / watch-out section
- **Intro tone:** Transparent and helpful, not scary. The reader should feel the clinic is on their side — honest about pitfalls so patients go in with clear eyes. Avoid making this feel like a legal disclaimer.
- **Risk items to cover:**
  1. Finance interest and fees are set by the provider — the total repaid may exceed the upfront price.
  2. Approval for finance or early super release is never guaranteed — it depends on the patient's personal circumstances.
  3. Health fund rebates vary — patients must confirm exactly what their cover includes, not assume.
  4. Written estimates are based on the individual case and can change if clinical needs change.

## Tone and compliance notes
- No guarantees on financial outcomes — never promise a specific repayment figure, rebate amount or approval outcome.

- Voice: plain-language, reassuring, honest about complexity. Avoid jargon. Avoid over-hedging to the point of being unhelpful.
- Reader profile: cost-anxious patient who has already decided they want surgery and is trying to work out if they can afford it. They need clarity, not bureaucratic language.
