### Eligibility / Who is suitable?
Standard BMI-threshold-as-starting-point framing:
- General guide: BMI 35+ typically suitable for a surgical assessment; BMI 30–34 may qualify when weight-related conditions are present. Starting points, not a final ruling; the surgeon confirms.
- BMI is part of the picture, not the whole — whole-person assessment (conditions, history, goals).
- Weight-related conditions that strengthen the case: type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, obstructive sleep apnoea, severe reflux, PCOS, fatty liver disease.
- A history of diets that did not hold is expected, not a mark against the reader.
- Mental health history is part of the assessment and never on its own a reason to be turned away.
- GP referral is the usual first step and gives access to Medicare rebates; direct contact also possible.

### Being honest about what surgery can and cannot do (risks)
- **Opening paragraph:** No surgeon can promise a specific result; be cautious of anyone who does. Surgery is a powerful tool that works alongside the patient's effort, not instead of it. Note honestly that the answer to many FAQ questions is "it depends on you, your starting health and the procedure".
- **Risk bullets (5 items):**
  - All surgery carries risk: bleeding, infection, blood clots, leaks, anaesthetic reactions
  - Bariatric procedures require lifelong changes to eating and ongoing follow-up
  - Some procedures require long-term vitamin and mineral supplementation
  - Loose skin after significant weight loss varies between individuals; may be addressable later
  - Long-term success depends on sustained dietary and lifestyle change; weight can return without it

### Cost paragraph
No dollar figures. Cover: cost depends on procedure, private cover and individual circumstances; the total is several separate fees (surgeon, anaesthetist, hospital); most procedures attract a Medicare item number; Medicare contributes toward surgical/anaesthetist/consultation fees but not the hospital stay or theatre (that is private cover's role); appropriate private hospital cover usually means a gap rather than the full fee; super early release on medical grounds and third-party payment plans are options some use; a clear, itemised written estimate is provided before any commitment.
