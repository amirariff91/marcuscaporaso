## Page purpose

This is the page a reader lands on when they are close to reaching out but still hesitating. Its job is to remove fear and friction from booking — reassuring the reader that a consultation is just a conversation, carries no obligation, and is the right starting point regardless of how certain or uncertain they feel. It should make calling or booking online feel like the obvious, low-stakes next step.

### What happens at a consultation

- ~3 paragraphs (180–250 words for this section)
- **Para 1:** Describe the first appointment as an orientation consultation — 30 to 60 minutes, relaxed and pressure-free. The team talks through health history, what has and has not worked before, and what the reader is hoping for. Emphasise the non-judgement tone.
- **Para 2:** Explain what the reader gets from the appointment: time to ask anything, explanation of different procedures, walkthrough of what recovery looks like, open discussion of costs and funding, and a chance to meet the team who would support them — before they make any decision at all.
- **Para 3:** Reinforce that booking is not a commitment to surgery. Many people attend simply to learn more, and leave with the information they needed regardless of whether surgery turns out to be the right path. The reader stays in control throughout.

The comparison note to weave in naturally: A GP referral is recommended but not required to book. A referral may unlock a Medicare rebate on the consultation and keeps the GP involved in care. If the reader does not have one yet, they can still book — the team can talk them through it at the appointment.

### Who should book a consultation?

- **BMI / eligibility note:** There is no minimum or maximum weight required before booking. A consultation is the right starting point for anyone considering weight loss surgery, even if they are not yet sure it is for them.
- **Bullet points to address — the reader should recognise their situation in at least one:**
  - Considering weight loss surgery and wants honest, clear information before deciding anything
  - Has tried other approaches and wants to understand whether surgery could help
  - Has questions about cost, recovery, time off work, or what life looks like afterwards
  - Has a GP referral, or wants to book first and sort the referral later
  - A family member or friend is considering surgery and they want to come along for support

Keep this warm and inclusive. No one needs to have made up their mind to deserve a consultation.

### How booking works (4 steps)

One to two sentences of practical guidance per step. Make it feel easy.

1. **Get in touch** — Call (08) 9332 0066 or book online. The Perth-based team will find an appointment time that suits, at either Booragoon or Mandurah. No pressure on the phone — just a friendly call to get booked in.
2. **Confirmation call** — After booking, the team calls to confirm appointment details, let the reader know what to bring, and answer any quick questions before the day. Easy to reschedule if plans change.
3. **Your consultation** — The 30 to 60 minute appointment: talk through your situation, learn about the options, discuss costs openly, and meet the team. No obligation, no pressure to decide on the day.
4. **Decide in your own time** — Take the information home and think it over. If you want to move forward, the team will guide the next steps. If it is not the right time, that is genuinely okay.

### What to bring and how to prepare

- **Intro paragraph tone:** Reassuring — nothing is required to attend, and the reader will not be turned away. But a few things make the consultation more useful by allowing the team to give tailored rather than general information.
- **Items to address (framed as helpful, not compulsory):**
  - GP referral letter (recommended, not required — may unlock a Medicare rebate)
  - List of current medications including doses
  - Any prior test results or relevant medical reports already on hand (blood tests, scans)
  - A written list of their own questions (easy to forget in the moment)
  - A support person — partner, family member, or friend — is welcome

## Tone and compliance notes

- **Medical advertising:** No guaranteed outcomes, no testimonials, no before/after claims. The consultation outcome is "clarity and information" — not a promised surgical result.
- **AHPRA/TGA:** This page is primarily transactional/informational so the highest risk is implicit promises. Avoid phrases like "you will love the results" or "our patients always feel confident after the consultation."
- **Voice:** Warm, unhurried, reassuring. This is the page a nervous person reads before making the call. It should feel like talking to someone kind. Avoid clinical distance and avoid over-selling.
- **Reader:** A Perth adult who has been thinking about weight loss surgery for a while but has been held back by uncertainty — about cost, commitment, judgment, or just not knowing what to expect. They need the page to meet them where they are and give them permission to just ask a question.
- **Key message to land consistently:** A consultation is a conversation. Booking is not committing. You stay in control throughout.
