## Page purpose
This page is the honest risk conversation the practice gives at consultation, written down for the Perth adults who Google their way to it before (or after) deciding on weight loss surgery. It exists to be balanced: no minimising, no hype, no reassurance-only. Every patient who reads it should come away understanding that weight loss surgery is a major operation that carries real risk, and that understanding those risks is part of a good decision — not a reason to hide from one. It is also the site's YMYL trust anchor: a genuinely candid risks page is what makes the surrounding procedure pages credible.

**AHPRA note up front (reads as rule for the writer, not page copy):** this is health advertising. Absolute requirements: no guarantees of outcome, no superiority or "safest/best" claims, no testimonials or before/after content, no minimising language ("rare", "don't worry"), no fear-driven language either. Frame every risk factually, note variability between patients, and return the decision to the consultation.

## Section guidance

### Hero
- **H1:** *Complications of gastric sleeve and weight loss surgery: what you should know* (primary keyword near the front).
- Subheadline, 2–3 sentences: state plainly that all weight loss surgery carries risk — bleeding, infection, blood clots and leaks among them — that this page explains the common and serious risks by procedure, how the team works to reduce them, and which symptoms should never wait for a scheduled appointment.
- Writer note: the honest tone must be visible in the first screen. Do not open by reassuring.

### "All surgery carries risk" opening (H2)
- ~120 words + 4-stat bar. **FS target (definition style).**
- Open with the plain statement: all surgery — keyhole or open — carries baseline risks. The types of baseline risk overlap, but their incidence and severity vary between procedures and patients. List the general risks as a clean bulleted set: **bleeding, infection, blood clots in the legs or lungs (DVT/PE), injury to nearby organs, reaction to the anaesthetic**. Then state that weight loss surgery adds procedure-specific risks on top, covered below.
- Per-section keyword: secondary "bariatric surgery risks" in H2; body mentions primary keyword once here.
- Stat bar: label these as typical ranges from the published literature, *with the citation named*. Do NOT write the specific numbers into the brief body.

### Short-terms risks: the first six weeks (H2)
- ~250 words, split **Early general risks** and **Procedure-specific early risks** (two H3s).
- General early risks expanded in one short paragraph each: bleeding (may need blood transfusion or return to theatre), wound or internal infection, blood clots (and what the hospital does to prevent them — early mobilisation, compression, medication), and anaesthetic risk (why every patient has a pre-operative medical assessment first).
- Procedure-specific early risks as three short bullet blocks:
  - **Gastric sleeve:** staple line leak (the most discussed early complication — what it is, that it usually needs further treatment, that the hospital team is equipped to manage it); bleeding from the staple line; stricture/narrowing.
  - **Gastric bypass (Roux-en-Y & mini):** leak at the join; blockage of the connection.
  - **Gastric band:** slippage, erosion into the stomach wall, port problems — and note these typically appear *later*, not in the first six weeks.
- Per-section keyword: "gastric sleeve leak" secondary in the sleeve H3; body uses "staple line leak" and "anastomotic leak" naturally.

### "What symptoms should I act on straight away?" (H2)
- ~150 words + a two-column table (symptom → what it may mean / what to do). **FS target (list style) and primary People-Also-Ask target.**
- Table rows: fever or chills; worsening or persistent abdominal pain; repeated vomiting or inability to keep fluids down; redness, swelling or discharge from a wound; pain, swelling or redness in the calf; **chest pain, breathlessness, collapse/fainting or suspected clot on the lung (pulmonary embolism, PE)**; black or bloody stools. The action column must direct chest pain, breathlessness, collapse/fainting or suspected PE to **call 000**; other rows should direct the reader to immediate contact with the surgical team or the nearest emergency department.
- Close with the plain instruction: chest pain, breathlessness, collapse/fainting or suspected pulmonary embolism (PE) = call 000. For other symptoms, contact the surgical team immediately or attend the nearest emergency department — not a wait-and-see approach. Phone **08 9332 0066** for the practice only for non-emergency concerns during business hours.
- Per-section keyword: none forced; this is written for patients, which is exactly why it wins.

### Long-term effects (H2)
- ~300 words, four H3 short blocks. **FS target (structured list).**
- **Nutritional deficiency** — why smaller stomach intake and (for bypass) altered absorption mean lifelong supplements and blood tests; the specific ones named: iron, B12, vitamin D, calcium; frame as a managed part of follow-up, not a hidden cost of the surgery.
- **Reflux / GERD after gastric sleeve** — new or worsening heartburn is a known, honest limitation of the sleeve; some patients need medication and a small number need further evaluation; mention it was weighed in choosing the procedure.
- **Weight regain after gastric sleeve** — state honestly that the stomach and appetite can adapt, some patients regain weight over years, and that this is a recognised reason for follow-up dietitian support and, in selected patients, the revision conversation (internal link).
- **Other long-term changes** — gallstones with rapid weight loss; **internal hernia after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB)**, a classically late complication that can occur at any time, including beyond the first six weeks; new, severe or intermittent abdominal pain, especially with vomiting, needs urgent assessment — contact the surgical team immediately or attend the nearest emergency department rather than waiting for a scheduled review; **hair thinning**, often temporary, but with multifactorial causes, so persistent loss needs assessment; loose excess skin after large weight loss (body change, not a complication — link to the skin removal page); and, for bypass, keep **early dumping syndrome** separate from **post-bariatric hypoglycaemia (PBH)**. Explain each separately, including symptoms and common triggers: early dumping occurs soon after eating, while PBH is a later low-blood-glucose condition. PBH can be more serious and may need clinical assessment, so do not frame it as something managed only with eating changes; dietitian support and tailored eating changes may form part of management.
- Per-section keyword: "gastric sleeve long term side effects" in H2; "gerd after gastric sleeve", "weight regain after gastric sleeve", "gastric sleeve side effects" used in H3s/body naturally.

### How the team reduces risk (H2)
- ~200 words, process-forward tone.
- The risk-reduction chain in order: pre-operative physician assessment and optimisation (diabetes, sleep apnoea, smoking cessation); choosing the right procedure for the patient in the first place (band, sleeve and bypass have different risk profiles — a reason eligibility and surgical advice matter); surgery at **Mount Private Hospital or Waikiki Private Hospital** with a team that performs this work routinely; multidisciplinary follow-up (surgeon, dietitian), with clear instructions for symptoms that arise between reviews.
- Careful compliance line: it is legitimate to explain what a good bariatric pathway routinely does to reduce risk. It is NOT legitimate to assert this practice's own complication rates, "safest", "lowest" or any outcome claim — write no comparative safety statement of any kind.
- Per-section keyword: "bariatric surgery complications" may appear once in body; primary keyword not needed here.

### Honesty about surgery and about the alternatives (H2)
- ~150 words. Tie-back paragraph: state that for every patient the decision is a genuine weighing up — surgery carries risks that medications and lifestyle programmes do not, and each option has its own risk profile; the surgeon's role is to explain them side by side so the patient decides with full information. No pressure framing, no "worth it" verdict sentence. Direct to consultation.
- Per-section keyword: "is gastric sleeve safe" answered in one calm sentence within this section (honest answer: it is a major operation with real risks, which is why suitability is assessed individually) — do NOT create a separate H2 for that question.
