### What is a mini gastric bypass?
- ~3 paragraphs
- **Para 1:** Define the procedure. Also called a single-anastomosis or one-anastomosis gastric bypass. A keyhole operation: the surgeon creates a long, narrow stomach pouch and joins it to a loop of small intestine further down. The patient eats less and absorbs fewer calories from what they do eat. Keep language plain — no jargon.
- **Para 2:** Address the name directly. "Mini" refers to the surgical technique, not the result. It is called mini because the operation uses one surgical join instead of two, making it shorter and a little less complex than a traditional bypass. The weight loss it produces is substantial and similar to a standard bypass for most people. This paragraph prevents the reader from underestimating the procedure.
- **Para 3:** Describe the keyhole approach — small incisions, laparoscope (thin camera), no large wound. This generally means less pain and a faster return to normal activity. Most patients are home within one to two nights.

**Comparison note to weave in:** Compared to a standard Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, the mini bypass uses one join rather than two. This can mean a shorter operation and simpler recovery, with comparable weight loss for most people. The right choice is assessed individually at consultation.

### Eligibility
- **BMI note:** Generally suitable for patients with a BMI of 35 or above, or BMI 30 or above with related health conditions such as type 2 diabetes, sleep apnoea or high blood pressure.
- **Bullet points to address:**
  1. BMI of 35 or above, or 30 or above with conditions including type 2 diabetes, sleep apnoea or high blood pressure.
  2. The patient has genuinely tried to lose weight through diet, exercise and lifestyle change without lasting success.
  3. The patient is ready to commit to long-term changes in eating and to lifelong vitamin and mineral supplements.
  4. There is no medical reason that makes surgery unsafe — assessed carefully before any procedure is recommended.

### How it works (6 steps)
Each step gets a short heading and 1–2 sentences of content direction.

1. **GP referral** — The reader's GP sends a referral. This links care back to the regular doctor and gives the higher referred Medicare rebate on consultations.
2. **Orientation consultation** — The reader meets their surgeon to discuss health, weight history and goals. The surgeon explains whether a mini gastric bypass suits them and answers questions honestly. No pressure to decide on the day.
3. **Pre-operative assessment** — The patient sees dietitians and, where needed, physicians. A pre-surgery diet to shrink the liver may be required to make the operation safer.
4. **Surgery** — General anaesthetic, keyhole incisions, at St John of God Murdoch or Subiaco. Usually around one hour.
5. **Hospital recovery** — Typically 1–2 nights. The patient starts on fluids, supported by nursing staff, and goes home with clear instructions.
6. **Ongoing support** — Regular reviews with surgeon and dietitian. Long-term follow-up and blood tests keep weight loss and nutrition on track. Frame this as a continuing relationship.

### Benefits (5 bullets)
Each bullet conveys one clear, qualified benefit. No superlatives or guarantees.

1. Significant, lasting weight loss — clinical averages of 60–70% of excess weight; results vary between individuals.
2. Strong improvement in type 2 diabetes for many patients, often reducing the need for medication (outcomes vary; any medication changes made in consultation with their doctor).
3. A simpler operation than a standard bypass — one surgical join, usually meaning shorter time under anaesthetic and a less complex procedure.
4. Keyhole surgery with small incisions — generally less pain and a quicker return to daily life than open surgery.
5. Reduced strain on joints, heart and breathing as weight comes down, and a lower risk of weight-related illness over time.

### Risks / honest section
- **Intro paragraph tone:** Every operation carries risk. The mini gastric bypass is generally safe in experienced hands, but it is real surgery. The practice will discuss the patient's personal risk in detail at consultation so the decision is fully informed. Keep the tone matter-of-fact and reassuring without minimising.
- **Risk bullets to address:**
  1. Bleeding, infection or blood clots — as with any major operation.
  2. A leak at the surgical join — uncommon, but may require further treatment if it occurs.
  3. Bile reflux — irritation from bile reaching the stomach pouch; occasionally requires revision surgery. This is a specific risk of the single-loop design that distinguishes the mini bypass from the Roux-en-Y and should be stated clearly.
  4. Long-term vitamin and mineral deficiencies if supplements and blood tests are not kept up.
