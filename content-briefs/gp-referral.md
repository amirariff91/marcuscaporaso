This page serves two audiences at once. Prospective patients arrive asking "do I need a referral, and how do I get one?" The page must answer both clearly, explain why a GP referral matters (Medicare access), and remove friction from the first step of the journey. Critically, every existing OSWA content page links here, so it carries internal-link weight and must convert intent into action (book, or call to arrange a referral).

## Section guidance

### Hero
- **Headline direction:** Name the topic plainly and anchor to Perth — readers should instantly know this page explains how referrals work for weight loss surgery here.
- **Subheadline:** Address both audiences in one or two sentences. Acknowledge the patient question ("how do I get started?") and the GP question ("how do I refer?"), and lead with the practical hook: a referral is the normal first step and gives the higher Medicare rebate on the specialist consultation. Warm, direct, no pressure.

### Stats bar (4 stats)
Factual anchors, not marketing claims:
1. **Medicare access / Rebates** — a GP referral gives the higher Medicare rebate on the specialist consultation (without one a lower rebate still applies)
2. **Referral validity / 12 months** — a standard GP referral to a specialist is valid 12 months, counted from the FIRST specialist appointment, not from the date written (MBS Note GN.6.16 — the referral period commences on the date of the first service covered by the referral; state factually)
3. **Hospitals / 2** — Mount Private Hospital and Waikiki Private Hospital
4. **Care team / 10+** — surgeons, physicians and dietitians

### What is a GP referral and why do you need one? (3 paragraphs)

**Paragraph 1 — What a referral is**
Define a GP referral plainly: a letter from the patient's GP asking a bariatric surgeon at Obesity Surgery WA to assess and treat them. Frame it as the standard, expected way the Australian system connects patients to specialist care, and as the normal first step for weight loss surgery.

**Paragraph 2 — Why it matters (Medicare)**
Explain the core reason: with a valid GP referral, a Medicare rebate applies to the specialist consultation at the referred rate; without one the patient can still be seen but a lower rebate applies. Most bariatric procedures separately have a Medicare item number — do not frame the referral as gating the procedure rebate. Keep it factual; no dollar figures.

**Paragraph 3 — The practical benefit**
Explain that a referral means the surgeon receives relevant history in advance, so the first consultation is informed and focused. Reassure that getting a referral is routine for a GP and is the expected pathway, not a hurdle.

### Comparison note (inline callout)
Practical, dual-purpose guidance: what a good referral should contain (current weight/height or BMI, relevant medical history, weight-related conditions, current medications, previous weight loss attempts), the 12-month validity of a standard GP referral, and the key distinction — you can self-refer and be seen; a GP referral gives the higher Medicare rebate on the consultation. This serves both patients and referring GPs.

### Eligibility / Who is suitable? (reframed as "what patients and GPs need to know")

- For GPs: what a referral should note (BMI, medical and weight history, comorbidities, medications, previous attempts)

### How it works (6 steps)
Reuse the established journey wording. Each step two to three sentences:
1. **GP Referral** — ask your GP for a referral; gives the higher referred Medicare rebate and lets the practice review history before meeting
2. **Orientation Consultation** — meet the surgeon, discuss history, options explained in plain language with no pressure
3. **Pre-operative Assessment** — physicians and dietitians assess health, screen for reflux/sleep apnoea, prepare safely
4. **Surgery** — FRACS surgeon performs the keyhole (laparoscopic) procedure at Mount Private Hospital or Waikiki Private Hospital
5. **Hospital Recovery** — recovery in hospital, usually a few nights (NOT "3–4 nights"), with nursing and dietitian support
6. **Ongoing Support** — dietitians and physicians stay involved long term

### Why going through a GP referral is worth it (benefits)
Five concrete, factual points. Cover:
- A referral gives the higher Medicare rebate on the specialist consultation (usually reduces what the patient pays)
- The surgeon receives history in advance, so the first consultation is focused
- The GP stays in the loop, supporting coordinated care
- It is the routine, expected pathway — no special process, no extra delay
- It lets the team match the patient to the right surgeon and pre-operative assessment

Avoid: "best", "leading", "world-class", and any language implying superior outcomes.

### Being honest about what a referral does and does not mean (risks)
**Tone:** Calm, direct, frank — builds trust by not overselling.

**Opening paragraph:** State clearly that a referral is the start of a conversation, not a guarantee of surgery; suitability is assessed clinically; no surgeon can promise a particular result; surgery is a tool that works alongside the patient's effort.

**Risk bullet points (4 items):**
- A referral does not guarantee surgery; the surgeon assesses clinical appropriateness
- All surgery carries risks: bleeding, infection, blood clots, anaesthetic reactions
- Long-term success depends on sustained dietary and lifestyle changes; weight can return without them
- Lifelong vitamin and nutrient monitoring is needed after some procedures; the team supports this

### Cost paragraph
Explain costs depend on the procedure, private health cover level, and individual circumstances. State that a valid GP referral is what gives the higher referred Medicare rebate, and that most bariatric procedures attract a Medicare item number. Note that patients with appropriate private hospital cover typically pay a gap rather than the full fee, and that early superannuation access is an option some use. Emphasise a clear written estimate is provided before any commitment. Do NOT quote dollar figures.

### FAQs

## Tone and compliance notes
- Both surgeons must be identified with full credentials (MBBS, FRACS) in every bio reference
- No outcome guarantees, no "you will lose X kg", no "guaranteed results"
- No patient testimonials or case studies — AHPRA prohibits testimonial-style claims for regulated health services
- Do not claim to be "the best", "leading" or "world-class" — comparative claims require substantiation
- All surgery risk information must be present and must not be minimised or buried
- Medicare item numbers and referral validity (12 months) may be stated factually — they are publicly available MBS information; do not quote dollar figures
- Do not invent fax numbers or email addresses for sending referrals; direct GPs to phone (08) 9332 0066 to arrange
- Recovery is "usually a few nights" — never "3–4 nights"
- Voice: direct, credible, clinical — this is a clinical practice, not a wellness brand
