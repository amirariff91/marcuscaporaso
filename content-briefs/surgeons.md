### Being honest about what surgery can and cannot do (risks)
**Tone:** Calm, direct, and frank. This section builds trust precisely because it does not minimise risk. It is not a legal disclaimer — it reads as a genuine conversation.

**Opening paragraph:** State clearly that no surgeon can promise a specific result, and that patients should be cautious of anyone who does. Describe surgery as a powerful tool that works alongside the patient's effort, not instead of it.

**Risk bullet points (4 items):**
- All surgery carries risks: bleeding, infection, blood clots, anaesthetic reactions
- Some patients experience loose skin after significant weight loss; this varies between individuals and may be addressable later
- Long-term success depends on sustained dietary and lifestyle changes; weight can return without them
- Lifelong vitamin and nutrient monitoring is required after some procedures; the team supports this

### Cost paragraph

Mention that early superannuation access is an option some patients use. Emphasise that a written, itemised estimate is provided before any commitment is made — no surprises. Do not quote specific dollar figures on this page.

## Tone and compliance notes
- Both surgeons must be identified with their full credentials (MBBS, FRACS) in every bio reference
- No outcome guarantees, no "you will lose X kg", no "guaranteed results"
- No patient testimonials or case studies — AHPRA prohibits testimonial-style claims for regulated health services
- Do not claim to be "the best" or "Perth's leading" — all comparative claims require substantiation
- All surgery risk information must be present and must not be minimised or buried
- Medicare item numbers may be mentioned factually (they are publicly available MBS information) but eligibility must be described as clinically assessed, not assumed
- Bariatric Surgery Registry can be mentioned as a quality indicator (it is publicly known information)
- Voice: direct, credible, honest — this is a clinical practice, not a wellness brand
